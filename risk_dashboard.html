      How certain is the VaR?

      With one year of data every VaR is an estimate. Resampling the daily losses 2,000 times gives a 95% range around each figure.

      How the three models compare

      Value at Risk (VaR) is the loss level that daily profit falls past on the stated share of days. Expected Shortfall (ES) is the average loss on those worst days.

      Is the loss pattern normal, and does it repeat?

      These tests decide how far the Normal-model VaR can be trusted and whether one day's loss predicts the next.

      Does one day predict the next?

      Correlation between the loss on one day and the loss some days later. Bars outside the shaded band are statistically meaningful.

      Backtest: did the VaR hold up?

      Kupiec test compares actual days past VaR with the number the confidence level expects. A p-value above 0.05 means the model passes.

      How the numbers are built

      Daily loss
      Each transaction's profit is net sale minus cost. Profit is added up by day, and the loss is the fall in profit from the day before.
      Historical VaR
      The loss level that daily losses stay under on 95% or 99% of days, read directly from the data.
      Parametric VaR
      The same level estimated from the average and spread of losses, assuming a Normal curve.
      Monte Carlo VaR
      100,000 daily losses simulated from a fitted Student-t curve, which allows heavier tails than Normal.
      Expected Shortfall
      The average loss on the days that fall past VaR.

      Reading the other measures

      Concentration index (HHI)
      Sum of squared revenue shares times 10,000. Below 1,500 is low, 1,500 to 2,500 moderate, above 2,500 high.
      Demand volatility
      Standard deviation of monthly units divided by their average.
      Store risk score
      Four indicators (low margin, discount rate, profit volatility, revenue share) scaled 0 to 1 and weighted by entropy, so indicators that differ more between stores count more.
      Stress test
      Stressed profit equals sales times (1 + sales shock) minus cost times (1 + cost shock).
      Data
      2,000,000 generated transactions across 22 stores for 2022. Results change if you load real sales data.
